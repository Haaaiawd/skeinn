## Verdict

**Tie on points, but I would trust Arm Y's approach for a multi-week project — for one specific reason, not on totals.**

Arm X is the efficient relay: 3 batons, 133 turns, three clean finishes, additive pivot, and an honest refusal instead of a plaintext fallback (`docs/HANDOFF.md` "Assumption recorded"). If the spec were frozen and the surface were a modern browser, X is the disciplined choice.

But the product delivers over `file://`, and X's crypto is a **conditional feature** on that surface — encrypted export simply doesn't work where `crypto.subtle` is absent. Y ships a fallback that makes the pivot *actually true* on the delivery surface, and — critically — Y's own e2e caught **two real data-loss bugs** ("Clear never persisted"; "IDB→memory wrote nowhere", HANDOFF §"Bugs found and fixed") that X's jsdom suite structurally cannot see, because X never runs a real browser over the shipped file. X's "48/48 green" is honest but narrow; Y's "54/54" plus `build.mjs --check` plus a real-Chrome tier is falsifiable in more places.

For a **multi-week** project, Y's evidence discipline wins: its HANDOFF explicitly lists four things that are *not done* (no golden fixture, no `RESULTS.md`, no CI, clipboard e2e) — that is the document a skeptical reviewer and the next baton both need. Y also **proved its continuity survived conditions X never faced**: an 80-call cap and a harness crash, each recovered from on-disk state. X's 3-clean-finish relay is untested against the failure that kills real multi-week relays.

The cost is real and I am not waving it off: Y burned **773 turns and 10 sessions for one clean finish**. That is a process that kept running out of budget and only survived because the tooling caught it. If Y's structured state dir ("task ledger/design/decisions + context-recovery command") were tightened so batons finish deliberately like X's, it would dominate outright. As built: **X wins on cost; Y wins on what survives contact with reality; for multi-week I take Y and put X's turn budget as a hard constraint on the next relay.**
